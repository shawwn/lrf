 The invention relates to a method by which the distance of objects (targets) can be measured according to the principle of pulse transit time measurement and a device suitable for this, as generically known from the 
 WO 2008/009387 is known.
 From the prior art, a variety of distance measuring devices (devices for optical distance measurement) are known which operate equipped with a pulse laser diode according to the principle of pulse transit time measurement. You will u. a. used in military technology, logistics, industrial measurement technology and safety technology and are designed for specific distance ranges according to the application.
 For evaluation of a received signal, generated from a transmitting pulse impinging on an object and a received pulse reflected by the object, various evaluation methods are known, such as the threshold value method and the sampling method.
 In the threshold value method, the electronic time measurement is started with the transmission of a transmitted pulse and stopped with the reception of the received pulse. The received pulse is detected, and thus generates a so-called threshold signal when the amplitude of an electrical received analog signal generated therefrom exceeds a predetermined threshold value. For comparative, timely detection, the amplitude of the received signal must be high enough to give a sufficient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the pulse shape of the received signal must have a high slew rate, and the comparator threshold must be above the amplitudes of possible noise spikes. After stopping the time measurement, the transit time is known and the distance can be determined in a known manner with the aid of the speed of light. For transmit pulses with, for example, a pulse width of 5 ns, a pulse power of 20 W and a pulse frequency of 1 kHz, the distance meter is considered eye safe and suitable for ranges up to about 100 m (near range), the measurement accuracy at about 5 cm and the Measuring time for a single-pulse transit time measurement in the range of μs is. To process the received signal without losses in pulse rise time, pulse amplitude and pulse width, the receiver bandwidth should correspond to the frequency spectrum of the transmit pulse and be at 150 MHz.
 In the sampling method, the analog received signal is sampled and a memory image is generated from the digitized samples, which represents a so-called sampling signal. From the number of samples starting with the emission of the laser pulse and ending with the detection of the received pulse and the length of a sampling interval, the transit time can be subsequently determined and the distance calculated. By accumulation, that is, repeated time-synchronous sampling and adding up of associated samples of successive received signals, the SNR of the accumulated memory image is substantially improved, thereby significantly increasing the range. The improvement of the SNR is proportional to the root of the number of accumulated measurements. For transmit pulses with, for example, a pulse width of 50 ns, a pulse power of 2W and a pulse frequency of 1 kHz, the distance meter is considered eye safe and is suitable for accumulation of the received signals for a range up to 3000 m (long range), the measurement accuracy at about 0, 5 m and the measuring time until reaching an evaluable signal / noise ratio is about 1 s. The receiver bandwidth for faithful processing of the transmission pulses is 15 MHz.
 Distance measuring devices operating with laser pulses are considered to be eye safe if the laser energy reaching the eye does not exceed the limit values assigned to the emission times. That is, given an assumed same number of transmit pulses of two pulse trains over a same period of time for an equal maximum amount of energy of a single transmit pulse the eye safety is still given.
 With distance measuring devices in which the signal evaluation is carried out with the Schwellwertmethode, advantageously a transmission pulse with the smallest possible pulse width and the highest possible pulse power is generated, while with distance measuring devices, where the signal evaluation with the sampling method, advantageously a transmit pulse with the longest possible pulse width, during this the analog received signal can be sampled multiple times, and low pulse power is generated. Given the given pulse power and pulse width, a maximum pulse frequency then results, taking into account the criteria for eye safety.
 If the distance measuring devices are intended for applications either in the near range or in the far range, the evaluation of the received signals generated by the received pulses is carried out with one or the other method. A laser pulse generator encompassed by the distance measuring device is correspondingly designed such that it generates advantageous transmission pulses for the respective method, and the bandwidth of the receiver is set for the respective method.
 Since there is no equally well-suited method for the evaluation of received signals, which are generated from received pulses from both the near and the far range, there is the need to use different measurement methods for evaluating the received signal, as for the near range, the threshold method and the far range is the sampling method.
 For example, in ship docking maneuvers in ports or in a battlefield surveillance, the distance range from a few meters to several thousand meters can range. Such a distance range which at least partially comprises the near range and the far range should be understood as a large distance range within the meaning of the invention.
 The problem is exacerbated when the objects to be measured can be both cooperative and non-cooperative, since their reflectivity differs by orders of magnitude.
 Non-cooperative targets (the surface of the target serves as a reflector) reflect, compared to cooperative targets (to which retroreflectors are attached), about a 1000-fold small fraction of the laser energy incident from the transmit pulse. Therefore, for non-cooperative targets, the transition between the near and far ranges is about 100 meters, while for cooperative targets it is about 1000 meters. 
 In applications for a large distance measuring range, in practice one often works with two different distance measuring devices, each of which uses one of the two evaluation methods.

 In the method disclosed here, laser light (hereinafter transmitted pulse) is emitted in the direction of a target object, which detects the light signal scattered back from the target object (hereinafter received pulse) and derives the distance to the target object. To derive the distance, the principles of the threshold value method and the sampling method are combined so that a received pulse can be detected and evaluated with an extended dynamic range of the distance meter. For the identification and temporal determination of the position of the received pulse, an evaluation according to the sampling method is carried out in parallel with the evaluation of an electrical received signal generated by the latter according to the threshold value method. The dynamic ranges of the threshold value method - lower dynamic range - hereinafter referred to as short range and the sampling method - upper dynamic range - hereinafter referred to as far range, overlap, but can also adjoin one another. Thus, different detection methods are used when detecting a received pulse for a received signal generated for a lower and an upper dynamic range. In the overlapping area, both evaluation methods are used in parallel, in particular at the same time, which should lead to an improvement of the signal evaluation.
 A distance measuring device (device) for carrying out this method comprises a laser transmitter for emitting laser light (hereinafter transmitted pulses), a receiver for detecting a light signal backscattered by a target object (hereinafter receive pulses), a signal evaluation unit with which at least one receive signal generated from a receive pulse Receiving time can be derived with different methods. This is the Signal evaluation unit designed so that it can derive the reception time from a same received signal via a first channel with the threshold value method and a second channel with the sampling method and from this can close to the distance to the target object.

The disadvantages of the described method and apparatus are as follows: 
 A compromise must be made so that the transmit pulse can be used for both evaluation methods. As explained, the threshold method uses a laser pulse with a high pulse power for a long range, with a short rise time for a high accuracy of measurement which is as short as possible so that it still has only a small energy content to ensure eye safety. In the sampling method, a laser pulse is used with the longest possible laser duration in order to obtain as many sampling points as possible for a dimensionally accurate memory image. The pulse power must be low, so that the pulse at high pulse width still has a small energy content for eye safety. To emphasize a received pulse from the noise, the sampling of several received signals is accumulated in a known manner. 
 These demands for method-specific optimized received signals are with the / according to the 
 WO 2008/009387 A1 described incompatible method and apparatus, since the transmit pulse for both evaluation methods is a same dimensionally stable laser pulse with the same parameters. Both methods can therefore not be used optimally, resulting in both cases in a lower accuracy, shorter range and higher measurement time. For a measuring device, which should be eye - safe, the short - range is limited to a shorter distance compared to single devices, which are specially designed for close range and work with the threshold value method, ie a higher measuring accuracy Threshold method is achieved compared to the sampling method is limited to a smaller near range. 
 The receiver must be adapted to the short, high transmission pulses when using the threshold method or to the long, low transmission pulses when using the sampling method. This means that a faithful transmission of the analog received signal requires a minimum electrical bandwidth. As the electrical bandwidth increases, the electrical noise increases. Thus, the signal / noise ratio of the analog received signal deteriorates, which also leads to the reduction of the measurement accuracy and range. 
 The threshold value method is a real-time method in which the measuring time is determined by the duration of the laser pulse. In the sampling method, the memory image of one or more received signals is indeed generated in real time, but the samples are used to determine the duration of the samples to simulate the shape of the analog received signal and compared with a software threshold, resulting in a much longer measurement time. If both evaluation methods are used simultaneously, the measuring time is determined by the considerably longer evaluation time of the scanning method.
